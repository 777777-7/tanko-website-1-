# Will cold email work on big companies? — 19 September 2026

Short answer: **cold email on its own, no. What you are describing is not cold
email, and that is why it can work.**

---

## The distinction that decides this

A cold email says *"we sell tool cabinets, please consider us."* It gets deleted.
Facilities managers at large plants get dozens a week.

**You are not sending that.** You can open with *"we already supply Samsung."*

That is **reference-led outreach**, and it is a different thing entirely. In
Malaysian E&E manufacturing, a Samsung reference is close to the strongest single
sentence you can put in a first email. It tells a facilities manager three things
at once, without you claiming any of them:

1. Your quality passed an MNC incoming inspection.
2. Your documentation and delivery discipline passed an MNC vendor audit.
3. Someone with more to lose than them already took the risk.

**That is the whole asset. Everything else in this document is about not wasting
it.**

---

## Before you send anything — two checks

### 1. Confirm you are allowed to name Samsung

**Many MNC supply agreements forbid naming them as a customer in marketing without
written consent.** Breaching that can cost you the account you are trying to
leverage. Check the supply agreement or purchase terms before Samsung appears in
writing anywhere.

**If you cannot name them,** the reference still works, just anonymised:
*"We are an approved supplier to a global Korean electronics manufacturer at their
Malaysian plant."* Anyone in the industry knows which one. You lose maybe 20% of
the force and none of the legal exposure.

### 2. Confirm it is Primaxs that supplies them

You said your uncle supplies Samsung. **Primaxs is the entity you are writing
from.** If the Samsung relationship sits with a different company — a related
entity, a joint venture, a personal arrangement — then *"we supply Samsung"* is
not a true statement about Primaxs, and it will unravel the moment someone asks
for a PO number or a reference contact.

**Get this straight before you use it.** If it is Primaxs, you have a weapon. If it
is not, you have a liability.

## Where this approach actually fails

**Not in the writing. In the addressing.**

The single biggest killer is sending to `procurement@company.com` or a website
contact form. Those route to a shared inbox, and a shared inbox at a large plant
is where unsolicited supplier mail goes to die.

**You need a named person.** For industrial storage that is, in order:

1. **Facilities Manager / Facilities Engineer** — owns the building fit-out
2. **Project Manager** for the new plant — exists only during construction, and has
   budget and urgency
3. **Maintenance Manager / Tool Room Supervisor** — owns the actual problem
4. **EHS Manager** — for lockers, chemical and flammable storage specifically

**Central procurement is the wrong first contact.** They will tell you to register
on a portal and nothing will happen. Go to the person with the problem, let *them*
tell procurement to onboard you. That reverses the flow and it is the only version
that works at speed.

**LinkedIn is how you find the name.** Search the company + "facilities" or
"project manager" + Malaysia. You do not need to connect — you need the name, then
guess the email format from any known address at that company.

## Malaysia-specific reality

- **WhatsApp outperforms email for follow-up.** Email to open the door, WhatsApp to
  keep it open. Your number is already on everything.
- **Face-to-face still closes industrial deals here.** The email's job is to earn a
  site visit, not a purchase order. Write it with that single goal.
- **Bahasa or English depends on the buyer.** MNC facilities teams: English.
  Local-owned factories and government-linked plants: Bahasa often lands warmer.
- **Follow up twice, then stop.** Day 0, day 5, day 14. After that you are a
  nuisance and you have burned the name.

## The play that is better than cold-emailing Samsung

**Do not only target the big companies. Target the companies that supply them.**

If you are an approved Samsung supplier, you can credibly approach **Samsung's own
tier-1 and tier-2 suppliers in Malaysia** with a line no competitor can copy:
*"We already supply into Samsung Malaysia. We understand the standard your output
has to meet."*

Those suppliers are themselves factories. They have tool rooms, ESD benches,
lockers and racking. **There are far more of them than there are Samsungs, they
expand more often, they decide faster, and they have no central procurement portal
to hide behind.**

This is the highest-return idea on this page.

## What I would actually do

1. **Check the Samsung naming permission and confirm the entity.** Everything
   depends on this and it costs one conversation with your uncle.
2. **Build a list of 20 named people**, not 20 companies. Facilities and project
   managers, from LinkedIn. Selangor, Negeri Sembilan and Penang first.
3. **Write one email, not a template blast.** Reference first, one specific
   product claim, ask for a 20-minute site visit. Nothing else.
4. **Target Samsung's supplier ecosystem, not just Samsung.**
5. **Add credentials to the site on 23 September.**

**Cold email is not the strategy. The Samsung reference is the strategy, and email
is just how you deliver it.**
