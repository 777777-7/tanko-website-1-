# Post template — Malaysian Facebook register

Built from actual Malaysian supplier pages, not from theory. The reference is
**CT Hardware** (89,000 followers, five Klang Valley stores, Malaysia's largest
tool retailer) — the format below is what they and the machinery trading groups
actually use.

---

## The single most important correction

**LinkedIn rules and Malaysian Facebook rules are opposites.**

| | LinkedIn | Malaysian Facebook / groups |
|---|---|---|
| Emoji | Never | **Yes — as section markers.** It is the native register. |
| Length | 1,300–1,900 chars | Short and scannable |
| Links | Costs ~60% reach | Expected, put it in |
| Bold headline | No | **Yes** — bold unicode headers are a local convention |
| Offer block | No | **Mandatory** — price, delivery, warranty, phone |
| Hashtags | Max 3 | 5–8 niche ones are fine |

Long-form essay posts are correct on LinkedIn and **wrong** in a bengkel group.
A workshop owner scrolling on a phone between jobs needs the offer visible in
two seconds.

---

## Why Facebook, in numbers

- **86% of Malaysian adults use Facebook.** Reach *grew* over the past year.
- The audience **skews older and slightly male** — which is exactly a workshop owner and a plant manager.
- **Language targeting is real:** Bahasa Melayu = broad mass reach; **Mandarin = high trust with Chinese-Malaysian SME owners**; English = corporate and urban B2B.
- Facebook does **not** reward hashtag overload — niche beats volume.
- Video/Reels get ~4.8x the shares of static images. Worth building toward.

That third point is why every post ships in three languages. It is not
duplication — they reach three different buyer populations.


**Photos: 3–4 per post, always.** A group post with no photo gets almost no
reach. Source from `asset3/` (2,151 jpgs, named by SKU). Never mix SKUs within
one post.

## Posting safely at volume

Wei Ming wants maximum volume. The constraint is real and worth stating once:
**a banned account reaches zero customers**, so volume has to be delivered in a
way Facebook does not read as spam.

What triggers a ban:
- The same text pasted into many groups in a short window
- Many groups joined at once (Facebook cut this off at 12 in one session)
- Links in every post, in every group, with no other activity

What delivers the same volume safely:

1. **Vary the copy per group.** Different pain, different opening line. The
   three languages give three genuinely different posts before any rewriting.
2. **Space them.** 15–30 minutes between group posts, not seconds.
3. **Match language to group.** Chinese post → Chinese hardware group. Malay
   post → bengkel group. This is both safer and better targeted.
4. **Match product to group.** Bengkel groups get tool cabinets and trolleys,
   never warehouse racking. `Iklan Bengkel-Bengkel Malaysia` rules say vehicle
   workshops only.
5. **Rotate.** Track last-posted dates in `PROMOTION-STATE.md` §4 and never hit
   the same group twice in a week.
6. **Comment before posting.** A genuine answer on someone else's thread first
   makes the account look human, and it is where the actual leads come from.

Roughly **6–8 group posts a day across different groups, in different
languages** is high volume and stays well inside safe limits. That is ~50 posts
a week, which is far more than the account can currently absorb anyway.
