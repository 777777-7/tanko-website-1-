## Notes on the draft

**The website is the argument, so it leads.** Every distributor who writes to a
Taiwanese manufacturer says they have good customer relationships. Almost none can
point to 1,851 pages, a number one ranking and a product feed and say *we built
that for the last Taiwanese brand we represented*. That is the paragraph that gets
this email answered, and it is why the URL appears twice — once in the body and
once in the signature.

**Stock is stated as a commitment, deliberately.** This is the opposite of the
Chung Fu letter, and the difference is the product. A cabinet lock costs a few
ringgit and a full range fits on one rack, so you can genuinely promise stock —
and stock is what earns a territory. Saying it plainly here is what makes an
exclusivity conversation possible later.

**The two questions come before the pitch.** Their export markets are not
published, so Malaysia may already be covered. Asking first is honest and saves
both sides time; it also signals you have done your homework rather than sent a
form letter.

**Samples are a fair ask** — their own website offers "unlimited MOQ, free
samples", so requesting them is taking up an offer they have already made, not
asking for a favour.

**No exclusivity is requested in this email.** Same reasoning as Chung Fu: you ask
for a territory after you have shown volume, not before. The stock commitment is
the groundwork for that conversation.

**One thing deliberately not claimed:** the email does not say storagesystem.com.my
already reaches panel builders. It does not — it ranks for storage terms. It says
you built that for Tanko and would build the equivalent for TAI SAM. Keep it that
way; they can check the site in thirty seconds and an overclaim would cost you the
relationship.

## Before you send

- **Their buyer is not your current buyer.** Tai Sam sells to people who *build*
  cabinets; you sell to people who *buy* them. Going in means learning the
  enclosure and switchgear trade. Worth knowing before they say yes.
- Confirm your Tanko agreement has no clause restricting other suppliers. Tai Sam
  makes no product Tanko sells, but check rather than assume.
- Decide in advance what annual volume and stock value you could realistically
  commit to, because question 3 invites them to ask.
