# Dealer Deal Registration Policy — draft for Wei Ming to issue

**Purpose:** stop several dealers quoting the same end-user with Tanko product and
undercutting each other on your price list. Written to be sent to dealers largely
as-is. Adjust the window and the discount language to match what you actually give.

**Why you can do this:** you hold the exclusive Malaysia distributorship. You set
the terms of trade. No platform, no fee, no permission required.

---

## PART 1 — The policy, as you would send it to dealers

> **PRIMAXS MARKETING (M) SDN BHD**
> **Project Registration Policy — Tanko Industrial Storage**
> Effective [DATE]
>
> **Why we are introducing this**
>
> We are the exclusive Malaysia distributor for Tanko Enterprise Co., Ltd. When
> several dealers approach us to quote the same end-user for the same project, the
> result is that our dealers compete against each other on our own price list.
> Nobody wins that: the dealer who does the work of finding and developing the
> project earns nothing extra for it, and Tanko's price in the Malaysian market is
> pushed down for everyone.
>
> This policy protects the dealer who brings us the project.
>
> **How it works**
>
> 1. **Register the project before you quote.** Email us with the end-user's
>    company name, site location, contact person, the products and approximate
>    quantities, and your expected decision date.
> 2. **We confirm within one working day.** If the project is not already
>    registered, we register it to you and confirm in writing.
> 3. **You receive project pricing, protected for 90 days** from the date of
>    confirmation.
> 4. **During those 90 days**, any other dealer who asks us to quote the same
>    end-user for the same project receives our standard list price, not project
>    pricing.
> 5. **Extensions.** If the project is still live at 90 days, tell us what has
>    moved and we will extend for a further 60 days. We will extend a genuinely
>    live project as often as it needs.
> 6. **First to register, not first to ask.** Registration is by date and time of
>    your email.
>
> **What registration does not do**
>
> - It does not reserve an end-user. It reserves **one project at one site**.
>   Another dealer may register a different project with the same customer.
> - It does not oblige you to buy, and it does not oblige us to hold stock. Stock
>   and lead times are confirmed on the quotation.
> - It does not apply to over-the-counter or catalogue orders below [RM ____].
>
> **If two dealers are already quoting the same project**
>
> Where a project is already in the market before this policy takes effect, we
> will register it to the dealer who first requested a quotation from us, based on
> our own records.
>
> **Register a project:** [email address] · Subject line: `PROJECT REGISTRATION —
> [End user] — [Site]`
>
> We will always tell you if a project is already registered. We will not tell you
> who registered it.

## PART 2 — Notes for you, not for the dealers

### The one rule that makes or breaks it

**You must actually hold the line.** The first time a dealer who did not register
pushes for project pricing and you give it, the policy is dead and your dealers
will know within a week. The policy costs you nothing to write and everything to
enforce inconsistently.

### Say this when a dealer complains

*"You get the same protection when you bring us a project. The alternative is that
you keep losing jobs to someone who did none of the work and just undercut your
quote."*

The dealers who complain loudest are usually the ones who were winning on price
rather than effort. The ones who develop customers will like this immediately.

### Keep the register simple

A spreadsheet is enough. One row per project:

| Registered | Dealer | End user | Site | Products | Approx value | Expires | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Sort by end user so a five-second lookup answers "is this already registered?"

### What to do when someone asks about a registered project

Tell them it is registered and quote list price. **Do not name the registered
dealer.** If they know who to call, they will try to buy through them or go around
them, and either way you have created a fight you did not need.

### Decide these before you issue it

| Setting | Suggested | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Protection window | **90 days** | 60 is common in IT; industrial sales cycles are slower |
| Extension | 60 days, repeatable | Ask what moved — it keeps dead projects out of the register |
| Minimum value | **[decide]** | Below this, no registration needed. Keeps small orders frictionless |
| Confirmation time | 1 working day | Slower than this and dealers will quote before you reply |
| Price gap | **[decide]** | The gap between project price and list price is what gives the policy teeth. Too small and it changes nothing |

**That last one matters most.** If project price and list price are nearly the
same, an unregistered dealer can still undercut the registered one and the policy
achieves nothing. The gap has to be wide enough that the registered dealer
comfortably wins.

### How this interacts with selling direct

You confirmed you can sell direct to end users. Keep the two consistent:

- **If a project is registered to a dealer, do not go direct on it.** That would
  make the policy worthless and your dealers would be right to be angry.
- **If an end-user has never appeared in your quote history**, it is open and you
  may approach it directly.
- New plants that do not exist yet are open by definition — nobody has registered
  a factory that has not been built.

### Rollout

1. Decide the five settings above.
2. Email it to all dealers at once, not one at a time. Same message, same day.
3. Give two weeks' notice before it takes effect, and register anything already in
   flight against your existing quote records.
4. Expect two or three arguments in the first month, then quiet.
