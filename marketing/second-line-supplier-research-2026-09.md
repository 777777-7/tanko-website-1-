## 2. Why I am NOT recommending the categories the demand data points at

This is the part that matters most, and it cuts against the obvious answer.

**Freight decides everything.** A container from Taiwan to Port Klang costs
roughly the same whatever is inside it. So the question for any category is:
what is the value per cubic metre?

- **Plastic containers and crates — don't.** Malaysia already has local injection
  moulders serving exactly this demand: Butterfly Plastic, Vanqo, EPE Pack, ABV
  Global (Simpang Ampat, Penang). They mould locally, ship 40km, and have no
  import duty or sea freight. You cannot beat that from Taiwan on a bulky,
  low-value part. Taiwan Harlin Enterprises (est. 1959) makes plastic crates and
  would probably talk to you — it still would not work.
- **Pallet racking — don't.** Local fabricators, heavy, price-driven, and the
  Johor/Penang queries are dominated by people who weld it locally.
- **ESD containers specifically — don't.** Same problem: bulky plastic, and
  Butterfly Plastic already lists ESD crates and anti-static containers for the
  Malaysian market.
- **ESD instruments (ionizers, wrist strap testers, resistance meters) — Taiwan
  is not where these come from.** The real players are Desco/SCS (US) and Chinese
  makers (Feita, Waterun, ESD Conductive). Taiwan is not the origin to pick.
- **Aluminium extrusion / modular framing — don't.** Heavy, and Malaysia has
  local extruders.

So the honest answer is: **your demand data and your best supplier country point
in different directions.** Chasing the Penang plastic demand would mean sourcing
from China or moulding locally, not Taiwan.

## 3. Where Taiwan is genuinely world-class AND you already have the customer

Taiwan's real industrial edge is **precision metal**: machine tool accessories,
workholding, tooling, hand and pneumatic tools. Taichung is a global centre.

And look at what you already own: **you rank #1 in Malaysia for CNC tool
storage.** The page that earns that traffic is read by tool room supervisors and
CNC shop owners — the exact people who buy tool holders, collets, pull studs and
angle heads every month.

You sell them the cabinet. You do not sell them what goes inside it.

That is the adjacency worth chasing:

| | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| Same buyer | The tool room already quoting you for an EA-series CNC cabinet |
| Same quote | Holders go on the same PO as the cabinet |
| Value density | A BT40 holder is small, heavy in value, cheap to freight |
| Spec-driven | Taper, runout, balance grade, gauge length — your site already sells on published specs, which is your genuine advantage over Shopee listings |
| **Repeat revenue** | Tanko is capex, bought once every several years. Collets, pull studs and retention knobs are consumables. This is the biggest strategic difference |

## 5. How to approach them

**Expect non-exclusive first.** Almost no manufacturer grants exclusivity to an
unproven partner on first contact. The normal path is: sample order → trial
period non-exclusive → exclusivity once you hit an agreed annual volume. Ask for
exclusivity as a *target with a volume trigger*, not as an opening demand. Asking
for it up front is the fastest way to be ignored.

**What you actually have to offer, and should lead with:**

1. Exclusive Tanko distributor since 2006 — you already run an import,
   warehousing, delivery and installation operation. That is the expensive part
   and you already have it.
2. Selangor warehouse, own installation crew, free delivery Selangor/KL.
3. **A website that ranks #1 in Malaysia for CNC tool storage**, 1,851 indexed
   pages, Google Merchant Center feed with 1,517 products, Business Profile. Most
   candidate distributors are a phone number and a shoplot. You can show a
   manufacturer a channel that already reaches their buyer.
4. An existing customer list of exactly their end users.

**Before emailing, verify each one yourself:**
- Search `"<brand>" Malaysia distributor` and `"<brand>" Sdn Bhd`
- Check their own site for a Distributors / 經銷據點 page
- Check newpages.com.my and Alibaba for Malaysian resellers already listing them
- Ask them directly — "do you currently have a partner in Malaysia?" is a fair
  opening question and their answer is the only reliable source

## 6. What I could not verify — read this before acting

- **Whether any of these companies currently has a Malaysian exclusive
  distributor is not public information.** I searched and found nothing for
  K-Gualda, Wey Juan, Hold Well, Bright Jing Chin or Jiahon — but absence of a
  web result is weak evidence, not proof. Only the manufacturer can tell you.
- **I have no margin data.** "High margin" here is inference from value density,
  technical content and the absence of marketplace price pressure, not from
  quoted prices. You will only know after you get export price lists.
- **I have not verified quality, certification or MOQ** for any of them.
- The demand figures are solid — they are your own Search Console data.
- The competitive findings (VERTEX/Secure Tooling; Butterfly, Vanqo, EPE, ABV in
  ESD and plastics) are from their own public listings and are reliable.

## 7. What I would actually do

1. **Test demand before committing to any supplier.** You are frozen on site
   changes until 23 Sep. After that, publish two or three guide pages on CNC
   tooling consumables — pull studs, collet selection, taper care — and see
   whether the CNC audience that already finds you converts on tooling queries.
   That costs nothing and answers the real question: will your channel sell
   anything other than storage?
2. In parallel, send the TAITRA trade inquiry and 3–4 direct emails, starting
   with Bright Jing Chin (pull studs) and K-Gualda (holders).
3. Ignore the Penang plastic container demand for now. It is real, but Taiwan is
   the wrong source and local moulders will beat you on it.
