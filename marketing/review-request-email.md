# Review request email — for customers who already bought

Keep it short. A review request that reads like marketing gets ignored; one that
reads like a person asking a favour gets answered.

**Send it 1–2 weeks after delivery and installation**, while the job is fresh and
the goodwill is still there.

---

## The email

> **Subject:** Quick favour — 30 seconds?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks again for your order — I hope the [product] is working well in the
> [workshop / store / lab].
>
> Would you mind leaving us a short Google review? It genuinely helps other
> Malaysian workshops find us.
>
> [REVIEW LINK]
>
> One line is plenty. Thank you.
>
> Wong Wei Ming
> Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd
> +60 11-5841 9886

**That is the whole thing. Do not add anything to it.**

---

## WhatsApp version — send this one instead if you have their number

> Hi [Name], thanks again for your order. Would you mind leaving us a quick Google
> review? Really helps us. One line is plenty 🙏 [REVIEW LINK]

**Reviews from WhatsApp convert far better than from email** in Malaysia. Use email
only where WhatsApp is not appropriate.

## Get your review link first

In your Google Business Profile, open **Ask for reviews** and copy the short link.
It looks like `g.page/r/XXXXXXXX/review` and opens the review box directly, already
signed in.

**Do not send people to your Maps listing and ask them to find the review button.**
Every extra tap loses a third of them.

---

## Why it is written this way

**"30 seconds" in the subject line** sets the cost before they open it. The most
common reason people skip a review is assuming it will take effort.

**"One line is plenty"** removes the second barrier — not knowing what to write.
You will get more reviews, and shorter ones, which is the right trade.

**No mention of stars, no "5-star", no incentive.** Asking for a specific rating
breaches Google's policy and can get reviews stripped or the listing penalised.
Offering a discount for a review is also a policy breach. Ask for the review, not
the score.

**Named sender, direct number.** It is a person asking, not a company broadcasting.

---

## Practical rules

- **Ask the person who actually dealt with you**, not a generic company address.
  The purchasing officer who spoke to you will write something; `info@` will not.
- **Space them out** — a dozen reviews landing in one afternoon looks manufactured
  and Google may filter them. A few a week is natural and sticks.
- **Never ask twice.** One request. If they do not respond, let it go.
- **Reply to every review you get**, positive or negative, within a few days.
  Response rate is itself a local ranking signal, and a calm reply to a complaint
  reads better to a prospect than no complaints at all.
- **Start with your happiest customers.** The ones who thanked your install crew,
  or reordered. Do not start by emailing the whole customer list.
