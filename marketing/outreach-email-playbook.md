# The outreach email — what to send, to whom, and why it works

**Samsung is confirmed: Primaxs supplies Tanko product to Samsung and the purchase
orders exist.** That is the credential this entire playbook is built on. It is
real, it belongs to Primaxs, and it is documented.

**One thing to check first:** whether your supply terms with Samsung permit naming
them in marketing. Many MNC agreements do not. If they do not, use Version B
below, which is anonymised and loses very little.

---

## The rule that matters more than the wording

**Send to a named person, never to `procurement@` or a contact form.**

A shared inbox at a large plant is where unsolicited supplier mail dies. You need:

1. **Facilities Manager / Facilities Engineer** — owns the fit-out
2. **Project Manager** (new plants only) — has budget and a deadline
3. **Maintenance Manager / Tool Room Supervisor** — owns the actual problem
4. **EHS Manager** — for lockers, chemical and flammable storage

Find the name on **LinkedIn**: company + "facilities" or "maintenance" + Malaysia.
Then work out the email pattern from any known address at that company — usually
`firstname.lastname@company.com` or `finitial.lastname@company.com`.

**Let the person with the problem tell procurement to onboard you.** That reversal
is the only version that moves quickly.

## Version B — when you cannot name Samsung

Replace the opening line with:

> **We are an approved supplier of industrial storage to a global Korean
> electronics manufacturer at their Malaysian plant, and to manufacturers across
> Selangor and Negeri Sembilan.**

Subject line becomes: **Tool storage for [Plant name] — approved MNC supplier,
stock in Selangor**

Anyone in the industry will work out who. You keep the credibility and lose the
contractual risk.

---

## Why this email is shaped the way it is

**The reference is in the subject line.** Facilities managers triage by subject.
"We already supply Samsung Malaysia" survives triage; "Industrial storage supplier"
does not.

**The specific reason is non-negotiable.** One true, specific sentence about *their*
site is what separates this from a blast. If you cannot write that sentence, you
have not researched the company enough to email it yet.

**The three bullets are their problems, not your features.** Lead time, crew
coordination, accountability. Nobody's day is improved by reading that you have
1,700 SKUs.

**"Made in Taiwan" sits in the body, not the opening.** It matters to an engineer
evaluating quality; it does not get an email opened.

**The ask is a site visit, not a quote.** In Malaysian industrial sales the
deal closes face to face. The email's only job is to earn 20 minutes.

**No attachment on the first email.** Attachments trip spam filters and nobody
opens a PDF from a stranger. The link does the work.

## Cadence

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | The email above |
| Day 5 | Two-line reply in the same thread: *"Bumping this in case it got buried — happy to just send specs if a visit is not useful."* |
| Day 14 | **WhatsApp**, if you have the number. One message, reference the email. |
| After that | Stop. Add to a 6-month list. |

**WhatsApp outperforms email for follow-up in Malaysia.** Email opens the door,
WhatsApp keeps it open.

---

## Sending rules — do not get your domain blacklisted

- **Send individually.** No BCC blasts, no mail-merge tool. Each one personally
  addressed and personally reasoned.
- **Cap it at 20–30 a day** from `sales@storagesystem.my`. A new-ish domain sending
  200 cold emails in a morning will land in spam permanently and you will lose the
  address for quoting too.
- **Never buy a list.** Malaysia's PDPA applies to commercial email, and bought
  lists are the fastest route to a spam complaint.
- **Reply-to must be a monitored inbox.** If a facilities manager replies in two
  hours and you answer in three days, you have wasted the Samsung card.

## Before the first send

1. **Confirm the Samsung naming permission.** One conversation with your uncle.
2. **Put credentials on the website.** A facilities manager will look you up within
   a minute of reading your email. Right now they find 1,850 excellent
   specification pages and **no evidence that anyone has ever bought from you.**
   The Samsung POs change that — even as *"supplier to a global electronics
   manufacturer"* without the name.
3. **Fix the Bing Places address.** They may try to navigate to you. Bing currently
   sends them to number 37 in the wrong village.

**Item 2 is the one that converts. The email earns the click; the website has to
survive it.** Site is frozen until 23 September — queue it.
