## The 0-connection gate — important structural finding

From a standing start, LinkedIn locks almost everything:

- **People search is anonymised.** Every result shows as "LinkedIn Member" with
  no name and **no Connect button**. Searching "procurement manager
  manufacturing" returned perfect targets — HP Malaysia, EMS buyers in Penang,
  a procurement lead who literally lists ePerolehan — and not one of them was
  invitable.
- **Alumni pages are the same.** 91,755 University of Malaya alumni, filterable
  by Engineering and Operations and Selangor, all anonymised, no Connect.
- **Only "People you may know" on `/mynetwork/grow/` can be invited.** That list
  is algorithm-chosen and refreshes over time.

So the sequence is forced, and it is slow by design:

1. Work the My Network suggestions daily — take the relevant ones, skip the rest
2. As first-degree connections build, second-degree opens up
3. Once second-degree is live, search becomes usable and real buyers become
   reachable with notes

**Cap: 15–20 invites per day.** A 0-connection account firing 100 requests is
the exact pattern LinkedIn restricts accounts for, and a restricted account is
very hard to recover.

**The fastest legitimate accelerant is importing Gmail contacts** — real people
who already know Wei Ming, which seeds first-degree fast. That needs him to do
it: it is his contact data and requires granting LinkedIn access.

**Post 1 — Load ratings**
```
"Heavy duty" is not a specification.

When someone asks for a heavy-duty workbench, the useful question back is:
what is the heaviest single item that will sit on it, and is that load spread
out or concentrated in one spot?

A 300kg engine block on a 100mm footprint is a completely different problem
from 300kg of parts spread across the whole surface. Catalogue load ratings
almost always refer to uniformly distributed load. Concentrated loads need a
different frame.

Get this wrong and the bench does not fail dramatically. It sags slowly, the
drawers stop closing square, and eighteen months later someone writes it off.

Full breakdown: storagesystem.com.my/guides/how-to-choose-a-workbench-malaysia/

#manufacturing #malaysia
```

**Post 2 — CNC taper damage**
```
A BT-40 holder can cost more than the cabinet that should be storing it.

Yet in a lot of machining shops they sit stacked in an ordinary drawer, tapers
touching, chips working into the sockets.

The taper is a precision surface. It is what locates the tool in the spindle.
One scratch or one chip trapped in there and you have runout — which shows up
as surface finish problems and shortened tool life long before anyone connects
it back to how the tools were stored.

Store them vertically, taper protected, in a socket sized for the interface.

storagesystem.com.my/cnc-tool/

#cncmachining #manufacturing
```

**Post 3 — Humidity**
```
Malaysian humidity does things to steel storage that catalogue photos never show.

Three failures we see repeatedly:

1. Lockers with ventilation only on the outer shell, not on each compartment.
   Damp work clothes go in, mould comes out.
2. Cabinets sitting flat on a floor that gets hosed down. The base is the
   first thing to rust and the last thing anyone inspects.
3. CNC holders stored with coolant residue still on them. Coolant plus
   humidity plus a polished steel surface is a rust recipe.

None of these are manufacturing defects. They are specification and habit
problems, and all three are avoidable.

#manufacturing #malaysia #maintenance
```

**Post 5 — Shadow boards**
```
A shadow board does not store tools any better than a pegboard.

What it does is make a missing tool visible without anyone running an audit.

That is the entire value. An empty outline is a check that happens every time
somebody walks past, at zero cost. A pegboard with tools hung on it looks
tidy; a shadow board with one gap in it tells you something.

The catch: it only works if missing tools get replaced quickly. An outline
that stays empty for a week teaches everyone that the system is decoration.
```

**Post 6 — Drawer slides**
```
The number one reason tool cabinets get replaced is not rust or damage.

It is drawer slides that start binding when the drawer is loaded.

Cheap slides are rated optimistically and tested empty. Load a drawer with
40kg of hand tools and a marginal slide starts to twist, the drawer sits
crooked, and eventually people just stop using that drawer.

Two things worth checking on any quote: the actual load rating per drawer, and
whether the slides are full extension or partial. Partial extension means you
cannot reach the back of a deep drawer without pulling the whole cabinet.

storagesystem.com.my/tool-cabinet/

#manufacturing #procurement
```

**Post 8 — ESD**
```
An ESD workbench that is not properly grounded provides no protection at all.

This is the most common finding in electronics audits we hear about. The
surface is correct, the wrist straps are there, and there is no verified path
to a common point ground.

ESD protection is a system, not a surface: dissipative top, common point
ground, wrist strap with series resistor, grounded frame, and ESD flooring
where operators stand. Miss one and the rest does less than people assume.

The damage you are preventing is mostly invisible. Partially damaged
components pass factory test and fail at the customer.

storagesystem.com.my/guides/esd-anti-static-workbench-malaysia/

#electronics #manufacturing #quality
```

**Post 9 — Locker locks**
```
Key locks or combination locks on staff lockers?

The answer usually comes down to headcount and shift pattern, not price.

Key locks are cheaper and fine when each locker belongs to one person
permanently. The hidden cost is administration — keys get lost, and every loss
is a call to someone.

Combination locks cost more upfront and remove the whole problem. No keys to
lose, and codes can be changed when staff change. For anywhere with shift
rotation or high turnover, they usually pay for themselves in saved admin time.

storagesystem.com.my/guides/steel-locker-buying-guide-malaysia/

#facilitiesmanagement #manufacturing #malaysia
```

**Post 10 — Mould racks**
```
Storing injection moulds on ordinary warehouse racking is one of the more
dangerous shortcuts in a moulding shop.

Moulds are deceptively heavy and the load is concentrated, not distributed.
Warehouse racking is rated for distributed load. A mould that looks compact
can put several hundred kilos through a small contact area.

Purpose-built mould racks have pull-out levels so a mould can be drawn clear
and lifted by hoist, rather than someone reaching into the rack. That is a
safety feature, not a convenience one.
```

**Post 11 — Bench height**
```
Bench height is not a detail. Over an eight-hour shift it is a fatigue and
quality issue.

Rough guide:

• Seated precision work — around 740 to 760mm, matching a standard chair
• Standing light work — 900 to 950mm, roughly elbow height
• Standing heavy work — lower, 850 to 900mm, so body weight can be used
• Inspection — higher, so nobody bends to see detail

If a station is shared across shifts by people of different heights,
adjustable is worth the cost. If it is one person every day, get the fixed
height right instead — it is cheaper and more stable.

#ergonomics #manufacturing #leanmanufacturing
```

**Post 13 — Aisle width**
```
The most expensive mistake in a racking layout is aisle width, because fixing
it means moving everything.

Working minimums:

• Manual picking only — 900mm
• Pallet jack — 1,500mm for a comfortable turn
• Counterbalance forklift — 3,000 to 3,600mm depending on truck and pallet

Measure the actual truck you run, not a generic figure. Turning radius varies
between models, and the difference between a comfortable aisle and a tight one
shows up as damaged uprights within a year.
```

**Post 18 — Maintenance**
```
Two things shorten industrial cabinet life more than anything else, and both
are avoidable in under an hour a quarter.

1. Neglected drawer slides. Dust and grit in the ball tracks make drawers
   bind. Wipe the rails and use dry silicone or a little white lithium grease.
   Not oil — oil attracts dust and turns gummy.

2. Untreated rust spots. Surface rust on a scratch spreads. Wire brush it and
   touch up the coating while it is still cosmetic.

A cabinet on this routine lasts 10-15 years in daily use. One that is only
fixed when it breaks does not.

storagesystem.com.my/guides/tool-cabinet-maintenance-longevity-malaysia/

#maintenance #manufacturing
```

**Post 20 — Stainless / food**
```
Stainless steel benches are specified for food and pharma for a reason that
```

**Post 21 — Modular workstations**
```
When is a modular workstation worth more than a plain workbench?

When the line is going to change.

A fixed bench is cheaper and perfectly fine if the task is stable. Modular
earns its cost when you run more than one product, when each station needs a
different combination of shelves, lighting and power, or when the layout is
likely to be reconfigured within two or three years.

The saving is not on day one. It is on the day the line changes and you
reconfigure the frames you already own instead of buying furniture again.
```

```
Lead time is a real cost even though it never appears on an invoice.
```

## Post format — researched 5 Sep 2026, supersedes earlier guidance

Two things I got wrong in the first two posts, corrected here.

### What the 2026 data says

| Format | Relative reach | Engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Document carousel | 2-3x | 45.8% (top format) |
| Multi-image post | strong | 6.6% |
| Text only | 1.5-2x | ~4% |
| **Single image** | 0.8-1.2x | 4.85% |
| **External link in body** | **0.5-0.8x** | - |

- **Single-image posts now UNDERPERFORM text-only by about 30%.** This reversed
  the 2024-2025 pattern. One photo is worse than no photo; three or more is
  better than either.
- **A link in the post body costs roughly 60% of reach**, and the old
  "put the link in the first comment" workaround is **also penalised** as of
  early 2026. That trick no longer works.
- **Hook must land in 210 characters.** 60-70% of readers never click "See more".
- **1,300-1,900 characters** is the sweet spot. Under 500 gets flagged as
  low-effort.
- **Three hashtags maximum.** Q1 2026 data: posts with more than three
  performed 71% worse than posts with none at all.
- **The first 60-90 minutes decide about 70% of total reach**, so early
  comments matter more than anything done later.

### What this means for Primaxs

The first two posts (workbench load ratings, CNC taper damage) each carried a
storagesystem.com.my link in the body. That cost roughly 60% of their reach.
Not fatal on an account with 4 connections, but it must not become the habit.

**The standing format from here:**

1. Hook in the first 210 characters - a specific claim, not a preamble
2. 1,300-1,900 characters of genuinely useful content
3. **Three or more product photos**, not one, not zero
4. **No URL in the body.** The website is on the profile and the Company Page;
   let the content earn the profile click
5. Two or three hashtags, never more
6. Reserve link-in-body for occasional posts where the click genuinely is the
   point, and accept the reach cost knowingly

**Highest-value format to build toward:** a document carousel - a 5 to 8 slide
PDF such as "Choosing a workbench top: rubber vs laminate vs stainless", built
from photos in asset3/ and copy already written in the guides. Top reach
multiplier and top engagement, and it showcases product visually without a
link.
