## 1. The new find — CHUNG FU TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT CO., LTD.

| | |
|---|---|
| Founded | **1977** — 49 years |
| Type | Exporter, Manufacturer, OEM, ODM |
| Certification | **CE and FM** |
| Export markets | China, Hong Kong, Japan, Macau, Vietnam |
| **Malaysia** | **NOT listed. Nor Singapore, Indonesia or Thailand** |

**Products:** FM Flammable Liquid Storage Cabinets · Class II Type A2 Biological
Safety Cabinets (3/4/5/6 ft) · Lab Bench · Zebrafish housing systems.

**Why this scores highest on competition and openness:**

- **FM Approval is a genuine moat.** Flammable liquid cabinets that carry FM
  approval are specified by name in insurance and HSE documents. You cannot
  price-shop an FM cabinet against an unapproved one — they are not the same
  product. That is the opposite of the air tool problem.
- **Biological safety cabinets are high-value capital equipment** (tens of
  thousands of ringgit each), bought by labs, pharma, universities, hospitals and
  semiconductor cleanrooms — and they require **annual certification and
  re-validation**, which is recurring service revenue, not a one-off sale.
- **Malaysia is genuinely absent from their export markets** — the cleanest open
  territory signal of anything found so far.
- Their Lab Bench line sits right next to what you already know about benches.

**The catch, and it is a big one.** Biosafety cabinets need trained service
engineers and calibrated validation equipment. You cannot sell one and walk away.
That is a real business to build, not a product to add. The flammable cabinet
line has no such burden — it is a steel cabinet with a fire rating — so **the
sensible move is to ask about the FM flammable cabinets only, and leave the
biosafety line unless you later want to build a service arm.**

Conflict check: Tanko makes no fire-rated or lab cabinets. But **their Lab Bench
could brush against Tanko workbenches** — worth excluding if you pursue this.

| K.Y.C. CASTER | 1979 | 10 | ASIA listed |
| CHUAN LUN | 1980 (SGS) | — | ASIA listed |

**HO Caster has the depth you asked for — 269 products** — including 2-tonne
heavy duty PU casters, elastic rubber wheels with stoppers, nylon wheels for
trolleys and 300kg platform trolleys. But they already reach Malaysia.

**JEIN YI is the open-territory candidate:** 1981, ~50 staff, exports only to the
US, Japan, Germany, UK and Canada. No Asian exposure whatsoever.

**Why casters fit you:** every trolley, workbench, workstation and mould rack you
already install rolls on them, they wear out and get replaced, they are a pure
spec sell (load per castor, wheel material, swivel/brake, bolt pattern), and the
buyer is your existing buyer. **Low operational burden — a caster never comes
back for repair.**

**Watch:** Tanko's trolleys and mobile benches already come with castors fitted.
Selling replacement and upgrade castors is a different market from supplying
Tanko's components, but mention it to Tanko rather than letting them discover it.

## 5. On low-competition B2C — my honest answer

You said B2C is acceptable. I would rank it last, and not because B2C is bad.

**Every asset that makes Primaxs worth something is B2B-specific.** A website
ranking for `tool cabinet malaysia`, a customer list of factories, an
installation crew, free delivery to Selangor industrial estates, and a
twenty-year name among engineers. In B2C none of that counts. You would be
starting from zero against Shopee sellers who are better at small-order
logistics, returns and paid ads than you will be in year one.

**And you already have the evidence.** `household-items` — your one consumer-
facing category — drew **37 impressions and zero clicks in 90 days**, the worst
page group on your site. That is the B2C experiment, already run, already
answered.

If you still want consumer revenue, the cheap version is to sell what you already
import to consumers through Shopee and Lazada — perforated boards and small tool
cabinets sell well to home workshops — rather than importing a new B2C line. Same
stock, new channel, no new supplier risk.

1. **Email CHUNG FU about the FM flammable liquid cabinets only.** Best margin,

   the knowledge you already have. Explicitly say you are not asking about

   nothing comes back, and it attaches to every trolley you already sell.

   quotes.
4. **Leave air tools until you have decided about a service bench.** They are
   fine businesses; they just cost you an operation you do not have.
