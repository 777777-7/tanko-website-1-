## 3. The RM450 decision — ANSWERED, see the full write-up

**MyProcurement loaded for the first time and I counted the whole thing.** The
numbers and the recommendation are in
[myprocurement-count-2026-09-19.md](myprocurement-count-2026-09-19.md).

**Short version: don't pay the RM450 yet.** 199 live items under your three codes,
only 28 storage-shaped, and almost none of those are actually your product —
130101 is school workshop teaching equipment, 020101 is wood and fabric furniture.
Only 24% publish an indicative price at all.

**But one live job matters:** UiTM Shah Alam wants a CHEMICAL STORAGE CABINET
(ACID AND FLAMMABLE), closing **28 September**. That is the Chung Fu product line,
in your own state, and you cannot bid it because you are not registered. That —
not volume — is the real argument for registering.

The original manual steps are kept below in case you want to check my counts.

**Step 1.** Open `myprocurement.treasury.gov.my/advertisements/quotation` —
Sebut Harga, the feed that suits you. There were **3,978 live items** on Thursday.

**Step 2.** Filter by **Kod Bidang `020101`**. Count the items that are genuinely
your product. Note the **Harga Indikatif Jabatan** on each and total them.

**Step 3.** Repeat for **`130101`**.

**Step 4.** Open `myprocurement.treasury.gov.my/results/quotation` — KEPUTUSAN —
for the same codes. Note who keeps winning and at what price. Expect four or five
names to recur.

**Then decide.** If the monthly volume under your codes is worth more than RM450
over three years, register. If it's thin, you've lost half an hour.

**One question to ask at registration:** does `130101` require a site
verification visit? You run a distribution warehouse, not a machining workshop.
Nothing about `020101` depends on the answer, and you can hold up to 30 codes on
one registration.
