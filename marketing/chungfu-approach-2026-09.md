# Approaching Chung Fu — the email, and three answers

---

## 1. "Can I hold no stock and just order when I get an order?"

**Yes for this product — but do not pair it with an exclusivity request.**

### Why zero stock is reasonable here

FM flammable liquid cabinets are configured capital equipment, not shelf goods.
Nobody stocks a spread of RM5,000–8,000 fire-rated cabinets speculatively, and
the buyer expects a lead time. Your own Tanko pages already say *"Not all
combinations are stocked — request a quote to confirm lead time"*, so this is the
model you are already running.

### Where it bites

- **MOQ.** A manufacturer may not accept a single cabinet, or will price one unit
  far above the carton price. Ask this explicitly — it is question 2 in the email.
- **Freight per unit.** One cabinet as LCL carries the same fixed charges as ten.
  This is what kills margin on back-to-back orders.
- **Lead time becomes your weakness.** If a competitor holds stock, you lose
  every urgent order.
- **It weakens the exclusivity case.** A manufacturer grants a territory to
  someone who commits inventory and builds demand. A distributor who holds
  nothing and buys only against orders looks like a trader. **Asking for
  exclusivity while offering zero stock commitment is contradictory, and an
  experienced exporter will spot it immediately.**

### The move that fixes most of this

**You already import from Taiwan.** Consolidate Chung Fu cabinets into your
existing Tanko consignments — same country, same port pair, same forwarder. That
turns an expensive one-off LCL into marginal cubic metres on a shipment you are
paying for anyway.

It is also a genuinely attractive argument *to Chung Fu*: you are not a new
importer learning the lane, you are an established one adding a line.

**So the honest position for the first email:** back-to-back to start, an
explicit intention to hold working stock of the fastest-moving sizes once demand
is proven, and territory discussed at that point — not now.

## 2. "Why would they choose me?"

Five reasons, in the order they will care about:

1. **Malaysia is not on their export list.** China, Hong Kong, Japan, Macau and
   Vietnam. You are offering a new territory, not asking to disturb an existing
   partner. That is the easiest kind of yes.
2. **Twenty years running exclusive distribution for a Taiwanese manufacturer.**
   Import, customs, warehousing, delivery, installation, after-sales — all
   already built. Most applicants are asking the manufacturer to fund their
   learning curve. You are not.
3. **An existing Taiwan → Port Klang lane.** No new logistics on either side.
4. **storagesystem.com.my.** 1,851 product pages, first in Malaysia for CNC tool
   storage, Google Merchant Center feed, Google Business Profile, full Bahasa
   Malaysia coverage. Almost every other candidate distributor in Malaysia is a
   phone number and a shoplot. You can show them a channel that already reaches
   industrial buyers, with evidence. **This is your single strongest
   differentiator and it should be in the first three paragraphs.**
5. **The right customer base already.** Manufacturers, tool rooms and industrial
   sites — the people who need flammable liquid storage.

## 3. "How long does it take them to send the product?"

Two separate clocks, and the shipping one is the small one.

| Stage | Typical | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Production, made to order | **30–45 days** | **The unknown — ask them.** Dominates the total |
| Sea transit Kaohsiung → Port Klang | **5–10 days direct** | Short hop; LCL 5–15 days |
| Customs clearance + inland to Balakong | 2–5 days | You already do this for Tanko |
| **Realistic door to door** | **~6–9 weeks** | Mostly production, not shipping |

Two things worth knowing:

- **Taiwan to Malaysia is one of the short lanes.** Direct Kaohsiung–Port Klang
  sailings run 5–10 days. Sanity-check against your own Tanko shipments — you
  already know this number better than any website does.
- **Production time is the figure that decides whether back-to-back works.** If
  they build to order in 45 days, your customer waits two months and you will
  lose jobs to anyone holding stock. If they hold finished goods and can ship in
  a week, back-to-back works beautifully. **Ask this first.**

### Notes on the draft

- **No exclusivity is requested.** Deliberate — see section 1. It is raised as a
  later stage, which is how these relationships normally work and makes you look
  experienced rather than hopeful.
- **No regulatory claim is made.** I have not verified what DOSH or Bomba require
  for flammable storage in Malaysia, so the email says "we do not see any
  FM-approved cabinet actively promoted here" rather than asserting a legal
  requirement. **Do not add a regulatory claim unless you have checked it** — an
  exporter with FM approval will know the standards better than either of us.
- The four questions are ordered so that question 3, production lead time, is the
  one that actually decides whether your no-stock model works.

## 5. Before you send

- Confirm your Tanko agreement has no clause restricting other suppliers. Chung
  Fu makes no product Tanko makes, but check rather than assume.
- Decide what you would say if they reply "yes, and our MOQ is one container."
  That is a real possibility and it is a different business decision.
