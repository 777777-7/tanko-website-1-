## LinkedIn — post now

> Most quotations for steel storage are compared on the wrong number.
>
> Buyers line up three quotes and look at the price. Fair enough. But the number that decides whether the thing is still in service in year eight is the load rating, and it is the one most suppliers here don't publish.
>
> Three questions that separate a real specification from a catalogue photograph:
>
> **1. Is that rating uniformly distributed, or concentrated?** A frame rated 600kg across the whole top is a different product from one that will take 600kg on a 100mm footprint. An engine block and 600kg of parts spread out are not the same load. Almost every published rating means uniformly distributed — ask.
>
> **2. Was it tested loaded or empty?** Drawer slides in particular. A slide rated optimistically and tested empty will twist under 40kg of hand tools, the drawer sits crooked, and eventually people stop using it. You paid for storage nobody opens.
>
> **3. What happens at the base?** In a washdown area or anywhere the floor gets hosed, a cabinet sitting flat wicks water underneath. That is the first place rust starts and the last place anyone inspects.
>
> The honest concession: for light assembly work a 600kg frame is genuinely the right answer, and paying up to 2,000kg is money better spent on the bench top or the lighting. Over-specifying is as wasteful as under-specifying — it is just less visible.
>
> We publish the load rating on every workbench and every drawer on our site. Not because it makes us look good on the cheaper models, but because a buyer who cannot compare the rating is comparing the wrong number.

⚠️ **Read this before posting.** Do not paste the identical text into six groups
in ten minutes. Facebook's spam detection keys on exactly that pattern, and the
account at risk is yours. Post to **two or three groups a day**, change the
opening line each time, and reply to any comments before posting the next one.

### Three things that went wrong, and the rules that come out of them

1. **The first attempt typed the whole post body into a comment box, not the
   composer.** `document.querySelector('div[contenteditable="true"][role="textbox"]')`
   matched the comment field of the post underneath the modal. Nothing was ever
   submitted and the draft was cleared, but Facebook persists comment drafts across
   a reload, so it took real ctrl+A and Delete on the focused field to remove.
   **Always scope the selector to the dialog:
   `dlg.querySelector(...)` where dlg matches `创建帖子`, never the document.**

2. **A JS helper reported success it had not verified.** It returned "clicked
   publish" and I read that as published, then reported six groups as done on that
   basis. **A helper must return the observed outcome, not the action attempted.**

3. **Checking a group's default feed does not prove a post is missing.** The feed
   sorts by relevance, and Page posts in moderated groups never appear at all.
   The reliable receipt is the green "已分享到你的小组" toast at the moment of
   publishing. **Screenshot immediately after clicking 发布 and read the toast.**

Also confirmed: a real mouse click at the publish button's computed centre works;
a synthetic `.click()` on it is unreliable in this dialog.
