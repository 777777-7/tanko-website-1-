# Three plans compared — Yung Chia, castors, Tai Sam — 17 September 2026

Wei Ming's shortlist. Researched chair.com.tw from scratch, then scored all three
against each other.

**Verdict up front: Tai Sam #1, Yung Chia #2, castors #3 — and Yung Chia is the
best *company* of the three while being the wrong *product* for you.**

## 3. The territory question — and I would not be optimistic here

Malaysia is not named anywhere in their export record. On its own that looks like
the same open-territory signal that made Chung Fu interesting.

**It is not the same, and the difference matters.**

Chung Fu has **no Southeast Asian presence at all** — Malaysia, Singapore,
Indonesia and Thailand are all absent, so nobody is covering the region.

Yung Chia has **its own sales offices in Singapore since 2019 and Thailand since
2018**, and is shipping to Bangkok. Malaysia sits directly between them.

**A Singapore sales office covering Malaysia is the standard arrangement in this
region.** The most likely answer to "is Malaysia open" is "our Singapore office
handles it." That is not certain, and it is worth one email to find out — but
treat this as **probably covered** rather than probably open, and ask before
investing any more thought.

| **Value per m³ freight** | **Poor** ❌ | OK | **Excellent** ✅ |

**Freight is what kills the chair plan.** This is the same value-per-cubic-metre
problem that ruled out importing plastics and racking from Taiwan in the original
research. A container of school chairs is mostly air, and it is competing against
**Malaysian manufacturers who pay no freight at all.**

School furniture also does not turn continuously. It sells in bursts — new
builds, refurbishments, term breaks — so you would carry bulky stock through long
flat periods. That is the opposite of the Tanko pattern you asked me to match.

## 5. The other two problems with the chair plan

### Wrong buyer, wrong sales motion

School furniture in Malaysia is sold to the Ministry of Education, state
education departments, universities, private and international schools, and
fit-out contractors. That is a **tender-driven, relationship-heavy, project
business** — not a catalogue business.

**Almost none of what makes Primaxs valuable transfers.** storagesystem.com.my has
no authority for school furniture and zero pages in the category. Your customer
list is factories. Your crew installs industrial storage. You would be starting
from zero and competing on tender terms.

### The competition already manufactures locally

Orange2you (partners with local manufacturers explicitly to meet government
standards), USL Educational Supplies (own factory), Multi Furniture Centre, MM
Iconic, Osaka Education, Officepro, Teyen — plus Chinese makers running
Malaysia-targeted pages. **Government procurement favours local content.** An
imported Taiwanese chair starts at a price disadvantage it cannot engineer away.

## 6. The one part of the chair plan that is genuinely good

**Factory canteen furniture.**

- Your customers **are** factories, and factories have canteens.
- **You have 0 pages for canteen, cafeteria or dining** — real white space.
- It is the same buyer: the facilities manager who already signs off your lockers
  and workbenches.
- Yung Chia's canteen range is real — 801H-1 table + 604P chair sets, 505 dining
  tables with benches, MFC tops, stacking chairs.

This is the only slice where your existing assets help, and it side-steps the
tender problem entirely because a factory canteen is a private purchase.

**But the freight maths does not improve** — canteen tables are just as bulky —
and local Malaysian canteen furniture is cheap and immediate. **Test it before
you import it:** quote canteen sets on your next three factory jobs, sourced
locally. If facilities managers bite, the demand is proven and *then* the import
conversation has numbers behind it. Exactly the same approach I recommended for
castors.

## 8. What I would do

1. **Tai Sam — first email.** Ask whether Malaysia is open and who buys their
   parts here now. Best freight profile, no competition found, no Tanko conflict,
   no MOQ.
2. **Chung Fu — send the email as drafted.** Opportunistic margin, verified open.
3. **Yung Chia — one email, low expectations.** Ask exactly one question first:
   *does your Singapore office already cover Malaysia?* If yes, stop there. If no,
   ask about the canteen range only, not school furniture.
4. **Castors and canteen sets — test locally before importing either.** Both are
   attach sales to customers you already have. Prove demand with local stock, then
   decide.
