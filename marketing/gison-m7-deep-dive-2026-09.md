# GISON and M7 — conflict check and deep dive, 17 September 2026

Two questions: do these clash with Tanko, and what do we actually know about
them?

---

## 1. Conflict with Tanko — checked against the real catalogue, not assumed

Tanko's Malaysian range is 11 categories and all of it is steel storage and
workplace furniture. Searching the whole site for tool and electrical terms:

```
air tool      0 pages        cordless     0 pages
pneumatic     0 pages        battery      0 pages
compressor    0 pages        power tool   2 pages (incidental text)
```

The only non-storage items Tanko actually sells are three workbench accessory
ranges:

| Tanko range | What it is |
|---|---|
| **WP-51 / WP-53** | **Bench vises** |
| WP-6 | Workbench seating, 4 styles |
| **WP-9003 / WP-9004** | **Ultra Thin LED work light** |

### The answer

- **GISON — no conflict at all.** They make air tools and nothing else. Tanko
  makes zero pneumatic products. There is no overlap anywhere in the range.
- **M7 — one narrow overlap: work lighting.** M7 carries a Lighting category and
  Tanko sells the WP-9003/9004 LED work light. That is a single line item out of
  six divisions and is easily carved out of any agreement — take the air,
  cordless, torque and socket divisions, leave lighting alone.
- **Watch vises generally.** Tanko sells bench vises (WP-51/53), so any future
  candidate offering bench vises would conflict. Note this is different from a
  *machine* vise for a CNC table, which Tanko does not make — that is why Hold
  Well's MC Precision Power Vise would not have clashed.

So: **yes, these are clean.** M7 needs one line excluded; GISON needs nothing.

### The Malaysian reality — this is the part that matters

**Hup Sheng Hardware (Selangor) carries 127 GISON products across 34
categories.** That is roughly a quarter of GISON's entire range, not a token
shelf. Categories include air angle grinder (10 models), die grinder (11),
impact wrench (13), orbital sander (14), hand drill (11).

But Hup Sheng describes itself as "the leading hardware retailer in Malaysia" —
**it claims no authorised distributor or dealer status anywhere on the page.**

So the position is: GISON sells into Malaysia in real volume, through a retailer,
with no exclusive arrangement visible. **That is not an empty market, and I
should not have implied it was.** What is missing is ownership — no local stock
commitment, no Bahasa product information, no technical support, no marketing.

## 5. Honest read

**GISON is the easier conversation but the harder market.** They advertise for
distributors, so the door opens. But Malaysia is not empty — a retailer already
moves a quarter of their range. Asking for exclusivity means asking GISON to
disturb a working relationship, and they may simply decline rather than upset
existing volume.

**M7 is the better prize but the harder door.** Bigger, better certified, and the
**cordless range matters** — that is where workshop tooling is actually going,
and an air-only line will look dated in five years. No visible Malaysian
presence. But a company at USD 10–50m turnover will have real requirements:
minimum annual volume, stock holding, likely a service centre, possibly a
showroom.

**Neither is a soft landing.** Both are mature companies who have been exporting
for forty years and have seen many distributor applications.

---

## 6. The question that should decide this before either email

**Are you willing to run warranty repairs?**

Air and cordless tools come back. Vanes, seals, bearings, triggers, batteries.
Customers expect a repair service, and a serious brand will ask what yours looks
like before granting anything. A Tanko cabinet has never once come back to you.

If the answer is no, then air tools is the wrong category regardless of how good
GISON and M7 look, and the direction to go back to is consumable tooling —
collets, pull studs, inserts — where nothing is ever returned.

Decide that first. It is a bigger decision than which brand to email.

---

## 7. What to ask them

Send both the same three questions. The answers tell you more than any research:

1. What is your current arrangement in Malaysia, and is any territory available?
2. What annual volume would you expect before discussing exclusive distribution?
3. What do you require of a distributor — stock holding, service centre,
   technical staff, showroom?

For GISON add: *we note Hup Sheng Hardware currently carries part of your range
at retail. We are asking about formal distribution, local stock and marketing,
not about displacing a retail customer.* Saying that up front is honest and stops
the conversation dying on a misunderstanding.
