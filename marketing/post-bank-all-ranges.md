## 1 · Workbenches → `/workbench/`
*893 SKUs · verified: 600kg Professional/Hexagonal, 2,000kg Heavy Duty, 45–50kg per drawer*

> "Heavy duty" is not a specification.
>
> When someone asks for a heavy-duty bench, the useful question back is: what is the heaviest single item going on it, and is that load spread out or sitting on one spot?
>
> 300kg of parts across the whole top and a 300kg block on a 100mm footprint are completely different problems. Published ratings almost always mean uniformly distributed load. Concentrated loads need a different frame.
>
> Our range runs 600kg on the Professional and Hexagonal frames and up to 2,000kg on Heavy Duty and Steel Top. Drawers are rated separately — 45 to 50kg each — and that number is the one people forget to ask about.
>
> Honest concession: for light assembly, 600kg is genuinely right and paying up to 2,000kg is money better spent on the top or the lighting. Over-specifying is as wasteful as under-specifying, just less visible.

## 2 · Tool cabinets & trolleys → `/tool-cabinet/`
*219 SKUs · verified: 400kg cabinet, 100kg standard drawer, 200kg T-drawer, 60kg/shelf*

> The usual reason a tool cabinet gets replaced is not rust and not damage. It is drawer slides that bind once the drawer is actually loaded.
>
> Cheap slides get rated optimistically and tested empty. Put 40kg of hand tools in, and a marginal slide twists. The drawer sits crooked, gets harder to close, and eventually nobody uses it. You paid for storage that stays shut.
>
> Two numbers worth asking for on any quote, neither of which shows in a photograph:
>
> The load rating **per drawer**. Standard duty is around 100kg; heavy T-drawers run to 200kg. A cabinet described as heavy duty with 100kg drawers is heavy duty in the frame only.
>
> Full extension or partial. Partial means you cannot reach the back of a deep drawer without pulling the cabinet off the wall.
>
> Cabinets themselves carry to 400kg, with a central lock across the whole stack — which matters more than drawer count if the cabinet moves between bays.
>
> Free delivery and installation in Selangor and KL, outstation quoted separately. Ringgit pricing, stock held in Selangor.

## 3 · CNC tool storage → `/cnc-tool/`
*90 SKUs · verified: BT-30/40/50, HSK-40/63, 400kg trolley, 100kg/200kg drawers, H1000 cabinet 1,000kg whole-frame*

> A BT-40 holder can cost more than the cabinet that should be storing it.
>
> Yet in plenty of machining shops they sit stacked in an ordinary drawer, tapers touching, chips working into the sockets.
>
> The taper is a precision surface — it is what locates the tool in the spindle. One scratch, or one chip trapped in there, and you have runout. That shows up as surface finish problems and shortened tool life long before anyone connects it back to how the tools were stored.
>
> There is a second problem specific to here: coolant residue on a polished steel taper, sitting in a closed cabinet in our humidity, rusts. A rusted taper cannot be repaired.
>
> Two habits fix most of it — clean and dry holders before they go away, and store them vertically in a socket sized to the interface rather than flat in a drawer.
>
> Our trolleys and cabinets hold BT-30, BT-40, BT-50, HSK-40 and HSK-63. Mobile versions carry 400kg, with 100kg standard drawers and 200kg T-drawers; the H1000 fixed cabinet is rated 1,000kg across the whole frame.
>
> Free delivery and installation in Selangor and KL. Ringgit pricing, Selangor stock, warranty handled from our office here.
>
> https://www.storagesystem.com.my/cnc-tool/

## 4 · Perforated tool boards → `/perforated-board/`
*113 SKUs · verified: 50kg panel, 6kg per hook, #304 stainless option*

> A shadow board only works if the shadow is drawn around the tool that actually lives there.
>
> Most 5S boards fail the same way. The board goes up, the outlines get drawn, and within a month someone hangs a different spanner in the wrong place because the hook nearest the bench was free. After that the board is decoration.
>
> Two practical things that decide whether it survives an audit:
>
> Hook rating. Ours carry about 6kg each. That is fine for hand tools and wrong for an impact wrench — and a hook that bends slightly stops holding the tool square, which is how outlines stop matching.
>
> Panel rating. The panel itself takes around 50kg. If you are hanging a full set, that is the number that matters, not the hook.
>
> Stainless #304 panels are available where the board is going somewhere that gets washed down.
>
> Honest concession: if tools genuinely live in a drawer and the bench is shared, a board adds a step rather than removing one. It pays off where one person owns one station.
>
> Free delivery and installation in Selangor and KL, outstation quoted separately.
>
> https://www.storagesystem.com.my/perforated-board/

## 5 · Modular workstations → `/workstation/`
*86 SKUs · verified: 100–200kg per shelf, 45kg/drawer, stainless or wood top, acrylic doors*

> Most warehouses I walk into have upgraded everything except the packing station.
>
> The racking is new. The scanners work. There is a WMS. And packing still happens on a folding table with one leg propped on a piece of carton. Then the operation wonders why packing is the bottleneck.
>
> Three things decide whether a station actually helps, and none appear on a quotation:
>
> Height matched to the task — 740–760mm seated, 900–950mm standing. Get it wrong and nobody complains, which is the problem. You just get a slightly slower shift, every shift.
>
> Shelf rating. Ours run 100kg per shelf, 200kg on the heavier frames. A shelf that sags is a shelf people stop loading.
>
> Consumables at the station. Tape, labels, knife, marker. If they live in the store room, every pack is a walk — two hundred times a day.
>
> Honest concession: if your volume is genuinely low and seasonal, a good table and a tidy shelf will do, and the money is better spent elsewhere.
>

## 6 · Mould racks & warehouse racking → `/rack/`
*10 SKUs · verified: 1,000kg per pull-out level, 500kg hoist rail*

> A mould shop usually loses more time finding a die than moving it.
>
> Dies on the floor, on pallets, two or three deep. It works until you need the one at the back — then it is a forklift, two people and twenty minutes for something you could have slid out by hand.
>
> Three things go wrong with floor storage and none appear as a cost:
>
> Dies stacked on each other chip on the parting line. You find out at the next setup.
> A die craned out of a stack is a two-person job every time.
> Concrete wicks moisture — in our humidity a die on the floor picks up rust underneath within a season, and the underside is the last place anyone inspects.
>
> Our mould racks take 1,000kg per pull-out level, and the heavier frames add a 500kg hoist rail so the die comes up rather than being dragged.
>
> Honest concession: under a dozen dies, all used weekly, floor storage is fine. A rack earns its keep when dies sit for months and still have to be perfect when they come out.
>
> Free delivery and installation in Selangor and KL, outstation quoted separately.
>

## 7 · Steel lockers → `/locker/`
*20 SKUs · verified: 1 to 18 compartments*

> Malaysian humidity does things to lockers that catalogue photos never show.
>
> The failure we see most is a locker ventilated on the outer shell but not on each compartment door. Damp work clothes go into what is effectively a sealed box at 80% humidity. Mould comes out. The unit is ventilated on the spec sheet and useless in practice.
>
> The second is the compartment count. Full-height lockers get ordered by default when what the line actually needs is a wallet locker — a small compartment bank for phones, keys and wallets at the entry point or gowning room, where a full locker will not fit and is not needed.
>
> Ours run 1 to 18 compartments. The right number is headcount per shift, not headcount total — a detail that quietly doubles orders when nobody asks.
>
> Honest concession: if your team keeps nothing on site, you need far fewer lockers than the floor plan suggests.
>
> Free delivery and installation in Selangor and KL. Ringgit pricing, Selangor stock, 1-year warranty from our office here.

## 8 · Parts cabinets & bin storage → `/parts-cabinet/`
*71 SKUs · verified: 5–10kg per bin, PS / ABS / PS+ABS*

> The question that decides how to store small parts is not how many you have. It is whether the picker has to look.
>
> Two different problems get solved by two different products, and they get mixed up constantly:
>
> High-value, slow-moving items — a lockable compartment. You want a record of who took it, and you want it to still be there next month.
>
> Fast-moving items — an open sloped bin at the right height, so a hand goes in without the picker breaking stride or reading a label. The moment someone has to stop and look, you have lost the time the bin was meant to save.
>
> Our bins are PS and ABS, rated 5 to 10kg each depending on size. Worth knowing: that is a plastic bin rating, not a steel one, and people do overload them with fasteners.
>
> Honest concession: bins without an address system are a tidier place to search, not a faster one. Number the bin, record the address against the SKU, or the investment does nothing.
>

## 10 · Document cabinets & A4 trays → `/documents-cabinet/`
*28 SKUs · verified: PS / ABS construction*

> Production paperwork has two states and they need two different products.
>
> Paper being **retained** — job cards closed out, certificates, traceability records held against a part or an order. That wants a compartment, ideally lockable, and it wants to stay put.
>
> Paper being **worked** — today's job cards, QC sheets waiting on a signature, anything moving between desks. That wants a tray, because a tray shows you the queue depth at a glance and a drawer hides it.
>
> The mistake is buying one and using it for both. Retained paper ends up in the tray where it grows into a pile nobody dares sort, and live paperwork ends up filed where it stops moving.
>
> Most production offices need both, which is why they sit in the same range.
>
> Honest concession: if your records are genuinely digital, you need a fraction of the filing you think — but you still need the trays.
>
