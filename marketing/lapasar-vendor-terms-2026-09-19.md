On 18 September I called Lapasar "a genuine channel, with a catch" and listed
their vendor terms under **Not verified**. I have now read the actual contract.

**The catch is bigger than the one I named.** I said the problem was margin — that
you'd be supplying another reseller. That is true, but it is not the clause that
should worry you.

## The three clauses that decide this

### 1. The penalty clause — read this one twice

> **9.3. The Penalty imposed shall be calculated at 50% to 100% of the total value
> of the Order** submitted by Lapasar's Clients accepted by the Vendor.
>
> 9.1. …Lapasar may impose a penalty on the Vendor for each Order, **at its sole
> discretion**, subject to any investigation made by Lapasar.

You get 7 days to justify and 3 days to appeal. But the ceiling is **100% of the
order value**, set at their discretion.

On a RM60,000 locker order, a dispute you lose costs you **RM30,000 to RM60,000 —
on top of having already shipped the goods.** That is not a normal marketplace
penalty, and it is the single most important thing on this page.

### 2. The fee is undisclosed and they can change it

> 6.1. A **Transaction Fee prescribed by Lapasar** shall be deducted from the
> Listed Price…
> 6.2. …should there be any changes to the Transaction Fee, it shall be
> communicated to the Vendor **at least 7 Business Days prior** to enforcement.
> 6.4. For selected vendors, **a Service Fee** described by Lapasar shall also be
> deducted…

**No number is published anywhere.** You cannot calculate your margin before
applying, and once you are in, the rate can move on seven business days' notice.
Both fees are "final and non-refundable in any event whatsoever".

**Ask for the Transaction Fee in writing before you list a single SKU.** If they
will not put a number in writing, that is your answer.

### 3. "Get paid in as fast as 1 day" is not the payment term

The marketing page leads with *"Get paid in as fast as 1 day after delivery."*

The contract says:

> **7.1. Lapasar shall make payment… within 30 to 60 days from the last day of the
> month in which the Orders have been GR.**

**From the last day of the month.** Deliver on 2 October and the clock starts
31 October — so 30 to 60 days after that is **early December to early January.**

The "1 day" is a separate opt-in facility where you submit proof of delivery and
*"Lapasar fronts the cash — subject to approval."* **That is factoring.** Nobody
fronts cash for free; ask what it costs.

## The operational terms — these you can actually meet

| Clause | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | Accept the order **within 1 business day**, or they may cancel it |
| 3.2 | Once accepted, **final and binding**; prepare for shipping within 2 days |
| 3.4 | Packed and sent to their client within **4–5 business days** of acceptance |
| 3.3 | If you cannot fulfil, you must decline in writing within 3 business days **with supporting documents** |

**You can hit 4–5 business days on anything you hold in Selangor.** That is what
your stock position is for.

**The risk is the 1-business-day acceptance window.** An order landing while you
are out on a site visit, unanswered for two days, is a cancelled order — and
accepting one you cannot actually supply is binding, which walks you straight into
clause 9.

## What I actually think

**Apply — but treat it as a stock-clearing channel, not a growth strategy.**

It costs nothing to apply. The buyer base is genuinely the hardest one to reach
any other way: GLCs and corporate procurement teams that will never answer a cold
email. For **fast-moving, in-stock, low-value items** where you know your cost and
can ship same-week, it is a real order flow.

**Do not put project work or made-to-order items on it.** A 200-locker fit-out
with a 6–9 week import lead time is exactly the order that trips clause 3.4, and
clause 9.3 makes that mistake cost half the order.

**Three things to settle before you list anything:**

1. **The Transaction Fee, in writing.** Plus whether a Service Fee applies to you.
2. **What triggers a penalty in practice** — ask for examples, not the clause.
3. **The cost of the 1-day payment facility**, if you would ever use it.
