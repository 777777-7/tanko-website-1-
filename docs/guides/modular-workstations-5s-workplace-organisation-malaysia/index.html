Modular Workstations & 5S Workplace Organisation for Malaysian Factories

By Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Exclusive Malaysia distributor for Tanko · Updated August 2026

On a lean shop floor, the workstation is where 5S either works or falls apart. A well-configured modular workstation makes the right tools obvious, the wrong ones absent, and the standard visible at a glance. For Malaysian manufacturers running 5S or lean programmes, the station design directly affects takt time and quality.

What is a modular workstation?

A modular workstation is a work surface built on a common frame that accepts add-ons — drawer units, pegboards, overhead shelves, task lighting and power rails. Unlike a fixed bench, it is reconfigured as the process changes. Tanko's Classic and Professional workstation lines are built this way.

How workstations support each S of 5S

  • Sort — drawer units and bins give a home to only the tools the station needs, making the excess obvious.
  • Set in order — pegboards and shadow boards put every tool in a fixed, labelled position within reach.
  • Shine — a clear, sealed surface is easy to wipe down and inspect.
  • Standardise — identical stations across a line make the standard visible and training faster.
  • Sustain — a missing tool shows instantly against its shadow, so drift is caught early.

Direct answer: To support 5S, configure each workstation so every tool has a labelled home within arm's reach — usually a pegboard or shadow board plus a small drawer set — and keep stations identical across a line so the standard is visible and audits are fast.

Configuring a workstation that improves flow

  1. Reach zones. Put the most-used tools and parts in the primary reach zone; secondary items on overhead shelves or lower drawers.
  2. Point-of-use power. Socket rails at the surface remove trailing leads and let the operator work safely.
  3. Lighting. Task lighting reduces defects on detailed assembly and inspection.
  4. Shadow boarding. A perforated board with tool outlines makes a missing tool obvious and speeds returns.

ESD and special environments

For electronics and E&E work — a major Malaysian sector — an ESD-safe surface and grounding protect sensitive components. For wet or chemical processes, a stainless surface is the right call. Match the top to the process, not the other way round.

Standardising a line

The productivity gain from workstations comes from making them identical. When every station on a line has the same layout, operators can move between positions, training is quicker, and 5S audits become a visual check rather than a paperwork exercise.

Frequently asked questions

How do modular workstations support 5S?

They give every tool a fixed, labelled home within reach (Set in order), make excess tools obvious (Sort), provide a clean surface to inspect (Shine), let you standardise identical stations across a line, and — with shadow boards — make a missing tool visible so the standard is sustained.

What is shadow boarding?

Shadow boarding is outlining each tool's shape on a pegboard or panel so its correct position is obvious. A missing tool leaves a visible gap, which speeds returns and makes 5S audits a quick visual check.

Can workstations be ESD-safe for electronics work?

Yes. For electronics and E&E assembly, workstations can be configured with an ESD-safe surface and grounding to protect sensitive components. Match the surface to the process — stainless for wet or chemical work, ESD for electronics.

Why standardise workstations across a production line?

Identical stations let operators move between positions, make training faster, and turn 5S audits into a visual check. Standardisation is where most of the productivity gain from modular workstations comes from.
