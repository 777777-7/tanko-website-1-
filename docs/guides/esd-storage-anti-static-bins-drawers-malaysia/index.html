ESD Storage in Malaysia: Anti-Static Drawers, Bins and Parts Cabinets

By Wong Wei Ming, Business Development Manager · Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Published 18 October 2026

An ESD workstation protects a board while someone is working on it. A lot of the damage happens before and after: in a cardboard box on a shelf, in a coloured plastic bin that charges up every time it is pulled out, or in a drawer that was never part of the ESD control plan. Air-conditioned production rooms in Malaysia dry the air, which makes static worse. This guide explains what anti-static storage is, what the resistance numbers mean, and which Tanko storage is made for it.

Direct answer: ESD storage is storage made from static-dissipative or conductive material, so components do not pick up a charge between workstations. For small parts, the Tanko TKI-2 "-9" cabinets have black anti-static drawers listed at 104 to 106 ohm, from RM325.35 for 20 drawers to RM1,156.80 for 75. For line-side bins, the TKI-83 hanging bins and KPQ-4302/4304 sets come in a black anti-static version for louvred and perforated boards.

Why storage is part of ESD control

A static-sensitive part spends far more time in storage and in transit than it does on the bench. Ordinary plastic bins and drawers are insulative: every time one slides out of a cabinet or is set down on another, friction builds a charge on it, and that charge has nowhere to go. A component dropped into it, or a hand reaching in, can discharge through the part. Low humidity makes this worse, which is why air-conditioned assembly and test rooms in Malaysia need storage that is part of the ESD control plan, not left out of it.

What the resistance numbers mean

ClassSurface resistanceWhat it does
Conductivebelow 1 x 104 ohmMoves charge quickly; suits shielding and grounded surfaces
Dissipative1 x 104 to below 1 x 1011 ohmBleeds charge away slowly and safely; the usual target for bins, drawers and work surfaces
Insulative1 x 1011 ohm and aboveHolds charge; keep it out of an ESD protected area

These classes follow the ESD Association's S541 packaging standard. Tanko lists the black TKI-2-9 drawers at 104 to 106 ohm, at the low end of the dissipative range. Your own ESD control plan sets the exact limits for your site, so specify against it and check with a surface resistance meter when the storage arrives.

Where each one fits

  • At the bench: anti-static hanging bins on a louvred or perforated panel above the workstation keep the parts for that station within reach and inside the ESD protected area.
  • In the component store: a TKI-2515-9 gives 75 labelled anti-static drawers in under 0.6m of wall.
  • For kitting and line feeding: the mobile TKI-2515-9M moves a full set of parts to the line on castors.
  • For high-value parts: the lockable TKI-2515D-9 keeps drives, boards and programmed chips behind a door.

Setting it up so it works

  1. Keep ESD storage inside the ESD protected area and keep ordinary bins, cardboard and bubble wrap out of it.
  2. Stand cabinets and hang bins on grounded ESD work surfaces or panels, so charge has a path to ground.
  3. Label the drawers and bins as ESD storage, so nobody tops them up with ordinary containers.
  4. Test surface resistance when the storage arrives and at the intervals your control plan sets.

Frequently asked questions

What is ESD storage?

ESD storage is shelving, drawers, bins and containers made from static-dissipative or conductive material, so they do not build up a charge and can bleed one away safely. It is used to hold static-sensitive components such as boards, chips and drives inside and between ESD protected areas.

Are black plastic bins automatically anti-static?

No. Colour says nothing on its own: an ordinary black bin can be just as insulative as a blue one. A bin is anti-static only if it is made from dissipative or conductive material. Tanko specifies its black TKI-2-9 drawers at 10^4 to 10^6 ohm surface resistance; check any bin with a surface resistance meter before it goes into an ESD protected area.

What surface resistance should ESD storage have?

Under the ESD Association S541 classification, dissipative materials measure from 1 x 10^4 to below 1 x 10^11 ohm and conductive materials below 1 x 10^4 ohm; anything at 1 x 10^11 ohm or more is insulative. Your ESD control plan sets the exact limits, so specify storage against it.

Can anti-static bins hang on a perforated board?

Yes. Tanko TKI-83 hanging bins come in a black anti-static version in five sizes and hang on louvred boards, or on perforated boards with the optional bin hanger. The KPQ-4302 and KPQ-4304 sets put four or three anti-static bins on a 420mm rail for perforated panels.

Yes. Delivery and installation are free in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur; outstation delivery, including Penang, Kedah and Johor, is quoted separately. A 1-year warranty against manufacturing defects is handled from our Selangor office.

Malaysia compliance notes

Industrial storage specified for Malaysian factories, workshops and campuses is typically governed by the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (OSHA) for aisle clearance and load ratings, the Factories and Machinery Act 1967 for storage adjacent to plant, and the local Uniform Building By-Laws (UBBL) for fire egress spacing. Malaysian standards (MS series) published by Department of Standards Malaysia apply to specific steel-furniture ratings. Enforcement sits with DOSH / JKKP for factory sites and Bomba for fire-code clearance. Confirm compartment weight limits and aisle widths with your facility's safety officer before deployment — Primaxs can supply Tanko's product data sheets for compliance sign-off on request.
