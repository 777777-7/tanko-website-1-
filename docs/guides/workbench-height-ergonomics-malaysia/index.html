Workbench Height and Ergonomics: How High Should a Workbench Be?

By Wong Wei Ming, Business Development Manager · Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Published 9 October 2026

There is no single correct workbench height. The right height is set by two things: what the operator is doing, and how tall that operator is. Get it wrong and the bench still works, but the person at it bends, reaches or shrugs all shift, and the back and shoulder complaints arrive a few months later. This guide turns the Department of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) 2024 standing-at-work guideline into numbers you can use when ordering a bench.

Direct answer: Set the work surface from the operator’s standing elbow height: about 5cm above it for precision work with elbow support, 5 to 10cm below it for light assembly, and 20 to 40cm below it for heavy work that needs downward force. Most fixed industrial benches are 750 to 850mm high, which suits heavy work standing or most work seated. Light standing assembly usually needs the work raised.

Why bench height shows up as back pain

A bench that is too low makes people bend at the waist and neck. One that is too high makes them lift their shoulders and wing their elbows out. Neither is dramatic on day one. Over a shift, and over months, the static load adds up. DOSH’s 2024 guideline points to the national figures for 2022: of 7,143 confirmed cases of occupational disease, occupational musculoskeletal disorders were the second largest group, with 678 cases.

The guideline’s first rules are the ones a bench height controls: keep the neck aligned, keep the elbows in and the shoulders relaxed, keep the wrists neutral, and keep everything within easy reach.

The elbow-height rule

Elbow height is the reference because it is where the forearms naturally work. DOSH puts the optimal working height of the hands “around elbow level” and says heavier work should be lower and precision or inspection work higher. CCOHS gives the numbers:

Type of workWork surface vs standing elbow heightExample at a 1,000mm elbow height
Precision: electronics assembly, inspection, writingAbout 5cm above, with elbow supportAbout 1,050mm
Light: assembly line, mechanical fitting5 to 10cm below900 to 950mm
Heavy: work that needs downward force20 to 40cm below600 to 800mm

The 1,000mm column is a worked example, not an average. Measure your own people: stand upright in work shoes, upper arm hanging, forearm level, and measure from the floor to the underside of the elbow.

“Work surface” means where the hands are, not the bench top. A 150mm fixture or a part in a jig raises the working height by that much. For heavy work, a vice adds its own height on top of the bench.

Sit or stand? Match the posture to the task

DOSH ties posture to the type of work:

  • Precision work may be done standing only for short periods, “preferably less than 10 minutes”. Longer precision and light work should be done seated, for visual and body stability.
  • Light work can be seated or standing. The table height is recommended near standing elbow height.
  • Heavy work should be done standing, because the force comes from the larger muscles of the shoulders, back and thighs. DOSH suggests a table height below the waistline.
  • Tasks with continuous foot control, such as a pedal-operated press, should be done seated.

DOSH’s checklist asks a blunt question: is static standing kept under 10 minutes, with leg movement or rest? If the answer is no for a whole line, the fix is a seat or a sit-stand arrangement, not a better mat.

Sizing a bench for several people

Most benches are shared across shifts. DOSH’s rule of thumb for reach is to design for smaller-statured people: if shorter operators can reach comfortably, everyone can. For height, it lists five ways to accommodate differences:

  1. Adjust the height of the work surface.
  2. Stand shorter operators on platforms.
  3. Set up a work area for tall people, and give short people a bench to stand on.
  4. Tilt the work surface.
  5. Use tool extenders.

In practice: buy fixed benches at the height that suits your shorter operators for the main task, raise the work for taller operators with a fixture or riser, and save adjustable benches for stations where operators change every shift.

Reach and bench depth

Height and reach go together. DOSH asks that items be placed around elbow height and within the reach of the forearm, about 50cm. Things in almost constant use belong inside that zone. Frequently used materials should stay within full-arm reach. Anything further forces a lean, and leaning over a bench loads the lower back as badly as bending to a low one.

  • Depth: 600 to 750mm deep benches keep the working zone within forearm reach. Deeper tops collect clutter at the back.
  • Back panels and shelves: put the tools used every cycle on a perforated board at the back of the bench, between elbow and shoulder height, not on a top shelf.
  • Parts: DOSH suggests smaller containers to shorten reaches. Tilted bins at the front edge beat a deep tote at the back.

Footrests, mats and stools

When the bench height cannot change, these three change the posture instead.

  • Footrests: DOSH says footrest heights between 10 and 30cm can improve muscle activity and posture, because they let a standing worker shift weight from leg to leg. A rail along the base of the bench does the same job.
  • Anti-fatigue mats: DOSH cites 11 to 11.5mm mats of medium to high stiffness as giving a significant reduction in lower-leg muscle activity. Softer is not better. DOSH also warns that mats can cause tripping, so they suit stations where people move little.
  • Sit-stand stools and bench seats: a stool sized for the bench height lets precision and light work go seated, which is what DOSH recommends for anything longer than about 10 minutes. Tanko’s workbench seating comes in four styles from RM216.90, fixed or gas-lift, with or without a backrest.

DOSH’s clearance figures for standing work are worth checking on any bench with a closed front or drawers: at least 10cm knee clearance and a toe space at least 13cm high and 13cm deep, so the worker can stand close without leaning.

Measure before you order: a checklist

  1. List the tasks at the bench and mark each one precision, light or heavy.
  2. Decide sit or stand for each, using the DOSH rules above. Anything precise and longer than 10 minutes goes seated.
  3. Measure standing elbow height for the shortest and tallest regular operators, in work shoes.
  4. Work out the target working height for the main task, then take off any fixture or part height to get the bench-top height.
  5. Choose fixed or adjustable. One operator or a narrow height range: fixed. Rotating operators: adjustable, or fixed plus platforms and risers.
  6. Check reach and depth: everything used every cycle within about 50cm.
  7. Add the extras the posture needs: stool, footrest or mat.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard height of a workbench?

There is no single standard. Fixed industrial benches are usually 750 to 900mm high. In the Tanko range they are 750mm (Performance WE 1200), 786 to 800mm (Heavy Duty WA and Professional WB), 810mm (Steel Top WD) and 836 to 850mm on castors (WA mobile). The right height depends on the task and the operator, measured from the operator's standing elbow height.

How high should a workbench be in mm?

Measure the operator's standing elbow height, then adjust for the task. Canada's CCOHS gives about 5cm above elbow height for precision work with elbow support, 5 to 10cm below for light assembly, and 20 to 40cm below for heavy work that needs downward force. For an operator with a 1,000mm elbow height that means about 1,050mm, 900 to 950mm, and 600 to 800mm.

What workbench height suits my height?

Use elbow height, not overall body height, because arm and trunk proportions vary. Stand upright in work shoes with the upper arm hanging and the forearm level, and measure from the floor to the underside of the elbow. Then apply the task offsets. If several people share one bench, size it for the shorter operators and give taller ones a raised fixture, as DOSH recommends.

Should workers sit or stand at a workbench?

It depends on the task. DOSH says precision work may be done standing only for short periods, preferably under 10 minutes, and that longer precision or light work should be done seated. Heavy work that needs force should be done standing. Tasks needing continuous foot control should be done seated.

Do anti-fatigue mats really help?

Yes, when chosen correctly. DOSH cites mats 11 to 11.5mm thick with medium to high stiffness as giving a significant reduction in lower-leg muscle activity. DOSH also warns that mats can cause tripping and suit stations where workers move little. Where mats do not fit, it suggests cushioning poured level with the floor, or cushioned insoles for mobile staff.

Is an adjustable-height workbench worth it?

When different people use the same bench, usually yes. A fixed bench is a compromise for everyone except the person it was sized for. The Tanko WKT-5102 packing station adjusts from 965 to 1,165mm. For fixed benches, platforms for shorter operators and raised fixtures for precision work are cheaper fixes.

Malaysia compliance notes

Industrial storage specified for Malaysian factories, workshops and campuses is typically governed by the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (OSHA) for aisle clearance and load ratings, the Factories and Machinery Act 1967 for storage adjacent to plant, and the local Uniform Building By-Laws (UBBL) for fire egress spacing. Malaysian standards (MS series) published by Department of Standards Malaysia apply to specific steel-furniture ratings. Enforcement sits with DOSH / JKKP for factory sites and Bomba for fire-code clearance. Confirm compartment weight limits and aisle widths with your facility's safety officer before deployment — Primaxs can supply Tanko's product data sheets for compliance sign-off on request.
