Steel Locker Buying Guide for Malaysian Factories, Gyms & Campuses

By Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Exclusive Malaysia distributor for Tanko · Updated August 2026

Steel lockers look simple, but the wrong specification creates daily friction — lost keys, damp interiors, too few compartments. For Malaysian factories, gyms and campuses, three decisions cover almost everything: compartment count, lock type and ventilation.

How many compartments?

Locker banks are specified by the number of compartments per column and the total footprint. The right count depends on shift patterns and what is being stored:

  • Fewer, larger compartments — where staff store bags, helmets or bulky PPE.
  • More, smaller compartments — for shift-based teams where many people need a small personal space, such as phone-and-wallet lockers.

Tanko's steel locker range is configured by storage-unit count so you can match the bank to headcount and space.

Direct answer: Size lockers to your peak headcount per shift and what people actually store. For shift teams sharing lockers, choose more small compartments with combination locks; for personal assignment, fewer larger compartments with key locks.

Combination lock or key lock?

Lock typeBest forTrade-off
Combination lockShift-based and shared use — no keys to manage or loseCodes must be reset between users
Key lockFixed personal assignmentKey management and replacement burden

For most factory and campus settings with rotating staff, combination locks remove the key-management headache. Fixed personal lockers — offices, long-term staff — suit key locks.

Ventilation and humidity

This is the detail most buyers overlook, and it matters in Malaysia. Enclosed steel lockers storing damp PPE, uniforms or gym kit will develop odour and condensation without airflow. Ventilated doors let interiors breathe and dry between uses. If lockers store wet or sweaty items, prioritise ventilation.

Placement and durability

  • Powder-coated steel resists knocks and cleans easily in industrial settings.
  • Leave clearance for doors to open fully in the aisle.
  • Sloping tops prevent items being piled on top and collecting dust where hygiene matters.

Where lockers are used in Malaysia

  • Factories — PPE, uniforms and personal items at shift change.
  • Gyms and sports facilities — member and staff day-use storage.
  • Campuses and schools — student and staff storage in changing rooms and technical areas.

Pricing note: Locker pricing depends on compartment count, lock type and quantity. Tell us your headcount and we'll quote a locker bank to suit.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the number of locker compartments?

Match compartments to your peak headcount per shift and what people store. Shift teams sharing lockers need more small compartments; staff storing bulky PPE or bags need fewer larger ones.

Are combination locks or key locks better for lockers?

Combination locks suit shift-based and shared use because there are no keys to manage or lose — you reset the code between users. Key locks suit fixed personal assignment. For rotating factory or campus staff, combination locks are usually easier to run.

Do steel lockers need ventilation in Malaysia?

Yes, especially where they store damp PPE, uniforms or gym kit. In Malaysia's humidity, enclosed lockers without airflow develop odour and condensation. Ventilated doors let interiors dry between uses.
