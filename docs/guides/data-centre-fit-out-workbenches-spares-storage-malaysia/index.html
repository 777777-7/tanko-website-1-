Data Centre Fit-Out in Malaysia: Build Benches, Spares Storage and Lockers

By Wong Wei Ming, Business Development Manager · Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Published 15 October 2026

A data centre budget goes on power, cooling and racks. The rooms around the data hall are furnished last: the build room where servers are staged, the spares store, the loading bay and the security entrance. They often end up with office furniture that is not ESD-safe, cannot take a loaded server chassis, and gives no way to count tools in and out of the data hall. This guide goes room by room through what those spaces need, with sizes and guide prices.

Direct answer: Beyond the racks, a Malaysian data centre needs five kinds of support furniture: steel build-room benches with ESD tops and grounding, multi-drawer cabinets for small spares, shadow boards and trolleys so tools are counted in and out of the data hall, lockers at the security entrance, and document cabinets for method statements. Tanko covers all five, with guide prices from RM168.70 (pegboard) to RM2,386 (heavy-duty bench), delivered across Malaysia.

Why this matters now

Malaysia is one of the fastest-growing data centre markets in Asia. Knight Frank's 2026 atlas puts Johor's live IT capacity at 1,110MW with a pipeline of 8,542MW, third in Asia-Pacific, and vacancy in Johor's co-location space has been reported at 0.7%. BMI counts 125 Malaysian data centres with 925.6MW live and around 2GW under construction. Every one of those buildings has a build room, a spares store, a loading bay and a security entrance, and those rooms are usually specified late in the project.

The build room bench: load, ESD and power

The bench in a build room has three jobs. It has to carry the heaviest thing you will open on it, so specify the frame for the kit, not for a monitor; Tanko heavy-duty frames are rated to 2,000kg. It has to be part of your ESD control: a dissipative top (typically 106 to 109 ohms surface resistance), a common-point ground and a wrist-strap point, so that charge drains away instead of reaching exposed boards. IEC 61340-5-1 is the standard most ESD control plans are written against. And it needs power and light where the work is, which is what a modular workstation adds: shelves, power rails and lighting that can be moved when the room is rearranged.

Tanko does not sell a single "ESD model": the ESD top and grounding are specified on the quotation, so they match your site's ESD control plan. Tell us the plan's requirements and we will configure the bench to them. See the ESD workbench guide for the five elements of an ESD workstation.

Spares storage that tells you what is in stock

A spares store works when every part has one labelled place. Multi-drawer parts cabinets suit small, fast-moving items: optics, screws, cable ties, fuses, rail kits. Drives and boards are best kept in their original anti-static packaging inside a lockable cabinet. The KPQ-4302 hanging bins come in an anti-static black version for loose small parts at the bench. Keep a separate, clearly marked shelf for faulty parts waiting to go back to the vendor, so they never return to stock by mistake.

Tool control at the data hall door

A tool left inside a rack or under a raised floor is a hazard nobody sees until something goes wrong. The simplest control is a shadow board at the data hall entrance: every tool has an outlined place, so an empty outline shows at a glance what has not come back. Pair it with a fitted tool trolley for the shift's kit, checked out and back with the permit.

Lockers at the security entrance

Many sites restrict bags and personal devices beyond the security point. Staff, contractors and vendor engineers need somewhere to leave them that is not the guard's desk. Steel lockers with a lock on every door and a name-card holder are the usual answer; plan for the peak number of people on site during a migration or a major maintenance window, not the normal headcount.

What to send for a quotation

  1. The rooms you are fitting out and their sizes (a floor plan is ideal).
  2. The heaviest item that will be opened on a bench, and how many build positions you need.
  3. Your ESD control plan requirements, if the build room is an ESD protected area.
  4. The spares you hold (types and rough quantities) and the peak number of people who will need a locker.
  5. The site address and the delivery window.

We reply with a layout and a written Ringgit quotation. Delivery and installation are free in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur, including Cyberjaya; outstation delivery, including Johor and Kulai, is itemised on the quotation. Frequently asked questions

What workbench does a data centre build room need?

A steel-frame bench sized for the heaviest kit you open on it, with an ESD-safe top and a common-point ground for anything with exposed boards, power at the bench and good task lighting. Tanko heavy-duty frames are rated to 2,000kg and modular workstations add shelves, power rails and lights. ESD tops and grounding are specified per quotation.

Are Tanko workbenches ESD-safe?

They can be configured for ESD work: a dissipative top (typically 10^6 to 10^9 ohms surface resistance), a common-point ground and wrist-strap points are quoted per project, so the bench matches your ESD control plan. The KPQ-4302 hanging bins come in an anti-static black version for small parts.

How should a data centre store spare parts?

Keep fast-moving small spares such as optics, screws, cable ties and fuses in labelled multi-drawer parts cabinets, keep drives and boards in their original anti-static packaging inside lockable cabinets, and keep a separate, labelled shelf for parts awaiting return to the vendor. Tanko parts cabinets start at RM289.20.

Do data centres need lockers?

Usually, yes. Many sites restrict bags and personal devices beyond the security point, so staff, contractors and vendor engineers need somewhere to leave them. Steel lockers with per-door locks and name-card holders suit a security entrance. Tanko lockers start at RM1,614.70.

Do you deliver to data centre sites in Johor and Cyberjaya?

Yes. Primaxs delivers across Malaysia. Delivery and installation are free in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur (which includes Cyberjaya); outstation delivery, including Johor and Kulai, is quoted separately on the quotation.

Malaysia compliance notes

Industrial storage specified for Malaysian factories, workshops and campuses is typically governed by the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (OSHA) for aisle clearance and load ratings, the Factories and Machinery Act 1967 for storage adjacent to plant, and the local Uniform Building By-Laws (UBBL) for fire egress spacing. Malaysian standards (MS series) published by Department of Standards Malaysia apply to specific steel-furniture ratings. Enforcement sits with DOSH / JKKP for factory sites and Bomba for fire-code clearance. Confirm compartment weight limits and aisle widths with your facility's safety officer before deployment — Primaxs can supply Tanko's product data sheets for compliance sign-off on request.
