Warehouse Racking, Mould Racks & Heavy Shelving — Malaysia Guide

By Primaxs Marketing (M) Sdn Bhd · Exclusive Malaysia distributor for Tanko · Updated August 2026

Heavy storage is the one category where getting the specification wrong is a safety issue, not just an inconvenience. Racking that is under-rated, overloaded or wrongly used can fail. This guide covers how Malaysian warehouses and tool rooms specify racking, mould racks and heavy shelving safely.

Start with the load — always

Every rack and shelf has a safe working load. The rule is simple and non-negotiable: know the weight of what you are storing, and choose racking rated comfortably above it. Two numbers matter:

  • Per-level load — the weight on a single beam level or shelf.
  • Total bay load — the combined weight the upright frame carries.

When in doubt, over-specify. The cost difference between duty ratings is small next to the cost of a failure.

Direct answer: Specify racking by the actual weight per level and per bay, choose a rating above your heaviest realistic load, mark the safe working load clearly, and never store concentrated point loads like moulds on shelving designed for distributed loads — use a purpose-built mould rack instead.

Why mould racks are different

Injection moulds and die sets are heavy and concentrate their weight on a small footprint. Ordinary shelving is designed for loads spread across the shelf; a mould places a large point load that can overload a shelf even if the total weight seems within rating. Purpose-built mould racks are engineered for these concentrated loads, in two- and three-column configurations, and often allow a hoist or crane to place and retrieve moulds safely.

Shelving for general storage

For boxed stock, tooling and general goods, steel shelving and pull-out racks keep items accessible and off the floor. Pull-out racks bring stored items forward for easy access, which suits heavy or awkward loads that are hard to reach at the back of a deep shelf.

Safe installation and use

  • Mark the safe working load on every bay so no one overloads it.
  • Load low and even — heaviest items on lower levels, weight spread across the level.
  • Fix and floor-anchor tall racking per the manufacturer's guidance.
  • Inspect regularly for damage to uprights and beams, especially near forklift traffic.
  • Keep aisles clear for safe access and material handling.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the right racking load capacity?

Work out the weight per level and per bay of what you're storing, then choose racking rated comfortably above your heaviest realistic load. Over-specifying is cheap insurance against a failure. Always mark the safe working load on each bay.

Why can't I store moulds on ordinary shelving?

Moulds and die sets concentrate their weight on a small footprint, creating a point load. Ordinary shelving is rated for loads spread across the shelf, so a mould can overload it even within the total weight rating. Use a purpose-built mould rack designed for point loads.

What is a pull-out rack?

A pull-out rack lets a stored level or unit slide forward for access, so heavy or awkward items at the back of a deep shelf can be reached safely without climbing or reaching over. It suits heavy tooling and dies.
